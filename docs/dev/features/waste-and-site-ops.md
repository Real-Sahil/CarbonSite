# Waste, site operations and evidence

Moved word for word out of CLAUDE.md so it is read only when working in this area (``lib/waste/`, `lib/fuel/`, `lib/material/`, `lib/evidence/`, `lib/plant/`, `lib/commuting/`, `lib/geo/``). CLAUDE.md keeps the rules that apply everywhere and an index of these files.

**Fuel stores** (`lib/fuel/`, sidebar → Calculations → Fuel, `/orgs/{orgId}/fuel?month=YYYY-MM&siteId=`, API `/api/orgs/{orgId}/fuel/stores[/{id}]` and `/fuel/entries` GET/POST/DELETE, migration `20261006000079`, plan in `docs/SITE_OPERATIONS_PLAN.md`): `FuelStore` (bowser, fixed tank or IBC with capacity, fuel type, owner, site, project), `FuelDelivery`, `FuelIssue` (to a register machine or a free-text vehicle) and `FuelDip`. **Receipts and bills stay the inventory**: these rows never create activity records, they allocate fuel to stores, machines and sites and check that the numbers agree. A dip is the level at the end of its day; `summariseStores()` (`rollup.ts`) expects the last dip to equal the previous dip plus deliveries less issues since, and flags a gap above 2% of litres issued (never under 20 L) as possible leak, theft or a missing entry; one dip gives no variance. Machines: litres issued beside telematics litres, hours and idle share; sites: litres delivered against approved diesel and HVO records (`recordedFuelBySite()`, shared with the Plant page). Editors are `PLANT_EDITORS`; every id is checked with `orgRefsError()` (`fuelStoreId`, `plantAssetId` were added to it); a store with history is retired (`active: false`), never deleted; entries can be removed and the audit log (`fuel.store_saved|entry_added|entry_deleted`) keeps them. **Field app** (`fuel_log` field submission, migration `20261006000080`, `lib/field-submissions/fuel-capture.ts`, Flutter `fuel_log_screen.dart`, Capture → Fuel Log): a worker logs a delivery (optionally with the delivery note photo, kept as the delivery's evidence), an issue to a register machine or a named vehicle, or a dip; the stores and machines of the site come from `GET /api/orgs/{orgId}/my-sites/{siteId}/fuel` (assigned sites only, cached per site for offline use). It goes through the normal review queue: `fuelSubmissionError()` checks at submission that the store is the organisation's, active and on the submission's site, approval (`approveFuelInTx()`, idempotent through `fieldSubmissionId`, no emission category, no calculation run) creates the entry and audit `fuel.entry_added`. The Dart screen was written without a Flutter SDK in the sandbox: run `flutter analyze` and a device check before releasing. Not built yet: fuel budgets, supplier feeds. Tests: `lib/fuel/__tests__`, `tests/security/fuel-tenancy.test.ts`.

**Material movements** (`lib/material/`, sidebar → Environment → Material movements, `/orgs/{orgId}/material-movements?siteId=&status=`, API `/api/orgs/{orgId}/material/{classifications[/id],movements[/id],files}`, migration `20261006000081`, plan in `docs/SITE_OPERATIONS_PLAN.md`): contaminated soil, asbestos, invasive species and other hazardous or controlled material, England and Wales first (carrier tiers CBDU/CBDL are the EA and NRW ones; Scotland and Northern Ireland differ, and the page says the competent person confirms the rules). `MaterialClassification` is per stockpile or source area (EWC code, hazardous flag and HP properties, laboratory reference, who classified it and when, planned route, evidence): `classificationBlockers()` (`checks.ts`) says what stops approval (EWC on the List of Waste, `*` and the hazardous flag agree, a named competent person and date, lab reference or file for contaminated soil, asbestos, hazardous and biological material), and editing an approved classification returns it to draft. `MaterialMovement` is one load (planned, dispatched, received, rejected, cancelled): **dispatch is blocked until the classification is approved** (409 `CLASSIFICATION_NOT_APPROVED`); every other gap is shown, not blocked, because a real load must still be recordable. **Receipt writes the existing `WasteRecord`** (`receiveMovement()`, idempotent through the unique `wasteRecordId`; needs a facility, a route and a ticket weight) and through `syncWasteRecordCalculation()` the s3-waste activity record and its calculation, so waste totals, diversion, the ESRS E5 hazardous split and carbon come from the one pipeline; edits to a received load mirror carrier, note, vehicle and destination onto the waste record. `movementChecks()` (pure, no model, no register lookup) reuses `checkDutyOfCare()` (EWC, carrier tier and expiry, transfer or consignment note, keep-until of 2 years or 3 for hazardous) and adds: classification not approved, no lab evidence, the destination's entered permit codes do not cover the load's EWC (or were not entered), ticket weight more than 10% off the plan, returned copy overdue against the organisation's own due date, nothing attached to a received load; each issue carries where to fix it. The platform shows whether evidence is complete and never says a load is lawful. Haulage carbon is not added to the inventory (the field `haulDistanceKm` is kept for it; DEFRA waste factors exclude transport, so it needs its own decision). Editors are `PLANT_EDITORS` (`MATERIAL_EDITORS`); every id is checked with `orgRefsError()` (`classificationId` was added) and evidence ids by `evidenceIdsError()`; a received load cannot be deleted or rejected, a classification with loads is withdrawn, never deleted; audit `material.classification_saved|movement_saved|movement_status|movement_deleted`. Not built yet: field app capture of loads (phase 2b), regulator register lookup, HGV movement caps from planning obligations, reminders (phase 3). Tests: `lib/material/__tests__`, `tests/security/material-tenancy.test.ts`.

**Site Waste Management Plan and carrier register check** (migrations `20261008000086`, from the SmartWaste comparison): **SWMP** (`lib/waste/swmp.ts`, `swmp-load.ts`, `SiteWastePlan`, `/orgs/{orgId}/waste/plan?projectId=`, `GET|PUT /api/orgs/{orgId}/waste/plans/{projectId}`, `POST .../approve`): one living plan per project (person responsible, principal contractor, client, forecast lines of waste type, optional EWC code, tonnes and planned route reuse/recycle/recovery/landfill, a diversion target, actions to reduce waste, review date). Statutory SWMPs were repealed in England, so the page presents it as a record clients, BREEAM and tenders ask for, never as a compliance claim: `swmpChecks()` lists what a reader looks for (7 items) and says so. Approval needs every check to pass (409 `PLAN_INCOMPLETE` lists the gaps), records who and when, and editing an approved plan returns it to draft as the next version. The page sets the forecast beside the project's `WasteRecord`s (`compareToForecast()`: a record goes to the line with the same EWC code, else the same waste type, the rest show as "Not in the forecast", planned and actual diversion). Project ids are checked with `orgRefsError()`; writers are `LINK_ISSUERS`, approval editors; audit `waste_plan.saved|approved`; print or save as PDF from the browser (no report type yet). Tests: `lib/waste/__tests__/swmp.test.ts`, `tests/security/waste-plan-tenancy.test.ts`. **Carrier register check** (`lib/waste/carrier-register.ts`, `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/waste/documents/{id}/check-carrier`, button "Check carrier register" on Waste documents): looks an England carrier registration number (CBDU or CBDL plus digits) up on the Environment Agency public register API (`environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/waste-carriers-brokers/registration.json?registrationNumber=`, no key, Environment Agency Conditional Licence which allows commercial reuse with the attribution "Contains Environment Agency information © Environment Agency and/or database right", shown beside every result, and no implied endorsement). Only the registration number is sent. The number is the licence's reference, or the carrier registration read from a transfer note. Results: on the register (holder, tier, expiry), expired, not found, unavailable (the register did not answer: never shown as "not registered") or not checked (a Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland number, which have their own registers). A not-checked result links to SEPA's, NRW's and DAERA's own search pages so a person checks by hand; none has an open API, and NRW's open dataset is an Excel file under an "NRW Conditional" licence that says NRW may not publish or disseminate the data freely, so it is not loaded. An exact number match is required. The result is kept on the document (`extracted.registerCheck`) with the date; the person still compares the holder with the carrier named on the document. Rate limited 30 a minute per user. Tests: `lib/waste/__tests__/carrier-register.test.ts`. **Wales shares the number space** (found 9 October 2026 from NRW's July 2026 workbook, 35,443 registrations since 2014, some in old `CB/AB1234CD/R` forms): Welsh carriers hold ordinary CBDU and CBDL numbers, and the England register does not list them, so a Welsh number is either "not found" or, worse, **belongs to a different English company** (CBDL3 is Gwynedd Forklifts in Wales and Seton Tree Care in Devon). The register-holder-versus-carrier-name check in triage is what stops the second case reaching "ready"; keep it. NRW's workbook is under the "NRW Conditional" licence (no free redistribution), so it is not in the repo or the product: ask NRW (opendata@naturalresourceswales.gov.uk) before loading it. **Permit and exemption check** (`lib/waste/permit-register.ts`, `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/waste/documents/{id}/check-permit`, button "Check permit register" on site permit and exemption documents; result kept as `extracted.permitCheck`): the same Environment Agency API and attribution, `waste-operations` for an Environmental Permitting Regulations number (two letters, four digits, two letters; `EPR/` prefix accepted) and `waste-exemptions` for `EXP/AP3041YF` (the slash form only; the hyphen form finds nothing). Shows holder, site, site type, the register's status ("Effective") or exemption codes and expiry; an exact number match is required; a failure is "unavailable", never "no permit". The register does not list which waste codes a permit covers, so a person still checks the site may take this waste. The `name` search parameter on the carrier register matches only the full holder name in capitals, and `companyNumber` is not a filter, so lookups are by registration number only. Tests: `lib/waste/__tests__/permit-register.test.ts`, `tests/security/waste-documents-tenancy.test.ts`. **Not built from the SmartWaste comparison, on purpose:** meter-photo and waste-note AI scanning (this deployment has no vision model; the text models cannot read a photograph, and Tesseract is not reliable on meter dials, so a wrong reading would enter the inventory), live carrier and software integrations (need partner agreements and keys), and cross-company benchmarks (need customer consent and a minimum cohort, a product decision).

**Carrier uploads become waste records, reminders and the monthly pack** (migrations `20261008000087`, `20261008000088`, from the SmartWaste comparison and pilot feedback): **Accept as a waste record** (`POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/waste/documents/{id}/accept-record`, "Record this load" on a transfer note, `WasteDocument.wasteRecordId`): a carrier sends a transfer note through an upload link, "Read file" suggests the fields (and checks the carrier registration on the England register when the file names one), and a reviewer confirms facility, period, waste type, route, weight and the rest; the record goes through the normal waste pipeline (`syncWasteRecordCalculation()`), so KPIs, the SWMP comparison and carbon pick it up. Human review is always on (no auto-accept). The document claim and the record create are one transaction (`wasteRecordId: null` in the where), so two reviewers cannot both make a record; a second attempt is 409 `ALREADY_RECORDED`; a transfer note reference already on a record is 409 `POSSIBLE_DUPLICATE` unless `allowDuplicate`; facility, period and project are checked with `orgRefsError()`; only transfer notes (422 otherwise). **Reminders** (`lib/waste/reminders.ts`, monitor `waste-documents`, Mondays 07:40 UTC, notification type `waste_document_expiry`): editors, sustainability and project/site managers are told about accepted licences, permits and exemptions ending within 30 days (stage `30`), within 7 (`7`) or lapsed in the last 30 (`expired`); `reminderStage()` decides; one notification per person, document and stage (the notification row is the record it was sent); a transfer note never lapses. **Upload links show quiet ones:** the link list shows the last file received and flags a live link with nothing in 30 days; a link's token is stored only as a hash, so MetricOra cannot resend the link by email: the team chases through its own channel. **Automatic intake: read on arrival, ready inbox, bulk approve, learned defaults** (`lib/waste/{read-document,triage,triage-load,accept}.ts`, no migration): a transfer note a carrier uploads through a link is read (PDF text or OCR) and checked on the England register in the background (`after()` in the public route, best effort; the Read button still works and shares `readWasteDocument()`). `loadTriage()` (three queries however many notes) gives each pending transfer note a `Triage`: `carrierDefaults()` takes facility, waste type, route, hazardous flag and destination from this carrier's earlier `WasteRecord`s with the same EWC code (matched on registration, else name; never across carriers or codes) and calls them consistent only when at least two earlier loads agree; `periodFor()` places the note's date in a reporting period. A note is **ready** only when the reader found reference, weight, date, EWC code and carrier registration, the register shows the carrier in date and its holder is the carrier the note names (`sameCompany()`, names compared like local-spend suppliers, a trading name inside the legal name counts; a mismatch means a wrong or borrowed number), the defaults are consistent, the weight is no more than 3x the median of that carrier's earlier loads, the date falls in a period and the reference is not already a record; otherwise it needs a look and the Waste documents page lists the reasons under it. "Ready to approve" (`ready-inbox.tsx`) lists ready notes with checkboxes and `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/waste/documents/bulk-accept` (max 50, `LINK_ISSUERS`) triages again on the server and records only the still-ready ones through `acceptTransferNote()`, the same claim-and-create transaction as the single accept route (one record per document; a second reviewer's attempt is skipped). Human review stays: a person ticks and clicks. Every acceptance audits `via` (review or bulk) and `changedFromSuggestion`, the number of fields the reviewer changed from what the reader and defaults suggested, so the reader's real accuracy can be measured before any auto-accept is considered (none is built). Prefilled defaults also fill the single "Record this load" form. Tests: `lib/waste/__tests__/triage.test.ts`, `tests/security/waste-bulk-accept.test.ts`. Not built: per-carrier email-in, a monthly digest, trusted-carrier auto-accept.

Set A (40) was used to write the rules, B (30) was written afterwards and also used to fix gaps, C (36) was never tuned against. They are not real customer documents, so the scores say how the reader does on these layouts, not on any customer's own paperwork: **run real notes through the inbox and read the edit counts before relying on it.** Before the corpus the reader read only 6 of 40 realistic notes fully right and turned "4,560 kg" into 0.005 t (commas were decimals); now it returns no wrong value on any set and misses one carrier name. What it found and fixed: thousands separators (comma then three digits is thousands, on kg), net weight beats gross and tare, "WTN-2026-381" stays whole, labels split from values by a tab or line break, ISO and "27-Feb-2026" dates, "Quantity (tonnes): 6.90", "Note number", "Reference", "Waste code" labels, "J. Patel & Sons" no longer cut at the initial, "Old Reg Haulage" no longer cut at "Reg", a carrier read from a letterhead only when the first line has no colon. **Safety rules in triage, so a bad read cannot reach "ready":** a note read from a photo (OCR) is never ready (look-alike characters: "A1" read as "Al", plate letters swapped; bordered tables in photos read as nothing, and cleaning the image first did not help, so it was not added); a weight over 44 t or under 0.05 t needs a look (a misread unit); a date more than two days ahead needs a look; a file with no waste code and no carrier registration is flagged "Does not look like a waste transfer note" (invoices and bills still yield a reference and a date); and **another note waiting in the inbox with the same reference** needs a look, with the same reference re-checked against waste records just before each insert in bulk approve (an end-to-end run found two pending notes with one reference both approved, counting a load twice). Checked end to end on a scratch Postgres with the real upload link, background read, inbox, bulk approve, scorecard and ask-the-carrier button (`CARRIER_REGISTER_URL` overrides the Environment Agency address so a local stand-in can answer in tests; leave it unset in production).

**Evidence seals and bundles** (`lib/evidence/seal.ts`, `bundle.ts`, `record-bundle.ts`, `verify-script.ts`, migration `20261006000082`): the digital evidence record for delivery notes, tickets and every other field photo. **Checksums are verified, not trusted:** the app sends each file's SHA-256 (it always did), and the server now reads the stored bytes back (`verifyEvidenceBytes()`): at submission (`POST /field-submissions`, 422 `EVIDENCE_CHECKSUM_MISMATCH`, audit `evidence.checksum_mismatch`) and again before approval (review and bulk review); a match sets `EvidenceFile.verifiedAt`; an unreadable file is not a mismatch. **Seals:** approving a field submission that creates an activity record calls `sealAfterApproval()` (never throws; the approval stands): `RecordSeal` (versioned per record, plain ids, immutable) holds a payload of the record's figures, supplier, description, site, facility and contract, every evidence file's id, SHA-256, size and whether the server verified it, what the device reported at capture (device time, GPS, SHA-256 of the OCR text), the reviewer, the time and the previous seal's hash; `sealHash` = SHA-256 of `canonicalJson(payload)`; the hash is also written into the audit hash chain as `record.sealed`, so editing the seal row, the record or a file afterwards is detectable. `GET .../activity-records/{id}/seal` says whether the record is sealed and `sealDifferences()` lists what changed since (amount, unit, date, supplier, facility, site, contract, files added, removed or altered); `POST` (reviewers and editors) seals again as the next version, linking to the one before; the record panel shows it with "Seal again". **Bundle:** `GET .../activity-records/{id}/evidence-bundle` (data readers, 10 a minute, audit `export.evidence_bundle`) streams a ZIP: the files, `evidence-index.csv` (recorded and actual SHA-256, match), `evidence-manifest.json` (files with the hash of the bytes actually in the ZIP, and the seals), `audit-log.csv` (the entries about the record, its submission and its files), `README.txt` and **`verify-evidence.mjs`** (zero dependencies: checks every file hash, recomputes every seal, checks each seal appears in a `record.sealed` audit entry whose own hash it recomputes; `lib/evidence/__tests__/seal.test.ts` runs it on real seals). **The assurance pack** now carries the same `evidence-manifest.json` and `verify-evidence.mjs`, and its `evidence-index.csv` and `manifest.sha256` use the hash of the bytes actually in the pack (they used to repeat the device's claimed checksum), with `actual_sha256` and `matches_recorded` columns and a README line counting mismatches; the verifier link gets it through the shared pack. **Limits, stated to users:** a seal proves nothing changed after sealing, not that the photograph was honest (device time and GPS can be spoofed, so the reviewer checking the ticket against the load still matters); records approved before this had no seal; the audit entries in a bundle are not a contiguous run of the chain, so the whole chain is checked with `verify-audit-log.mjs` from a pack; nothing outside the database anchors the chain's newest hash yet. Not built: sealing material movements and waste records, an outside timestamp anchor.

**Evidence tier and trace a figure:** `evidenceTier()` (`lib/data-quality/evidence-tier.ts`) labels each record Verified (primary data origin, evidence complete, approved), Partially verified (one missing, or reviewed secondary data with evidence) or Estimated; `summariseTiers()` weights by kg CO2e. Shown on the records table (`EvidenceTierBadge`), in the GHG Protocol report ("Evidence behind these figures"), as `data_origin`/`evidence_status`/`review_status`/`evidence_tier` columns in every CSV trail, and on `/orgs/{orgId}/lineage` ("Trace a figure": the published snapshot's headline split by tier, category totals from `CATEGORY_BREAKDOWN_DIMENSIONS`, then a category's calculations largest first with record, factor, formula, selection reason, warnings and evidence downloads; `GET /api/orgs/{orgId}/lineage`, paginated). Approved field submissions create records with `dataOrigin: invoiced` (migration `20260925000038` fixed older ones left at `estimated`).

**Add from a bill** (records page): `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/evidence/bill` stores the upload as evidence (`storeEvidenceFile()`, deduplicated by checksum), reads it (`documentText()` in `lib/imports/parsers/pdf.ts`: the PDF text layer through pdf-parse 2.x's `PDFParse`, else Tesseract OCR with the English data shipped in `@tesseract.js-data/eng` and traced into the function by `outputFileTracingIncludes`; never fetched from a CDN; `OCR_FILES` in `next.config.ts` ships Tesseract's worker, LSTM cores and data to every OCR route: the include keys are globs, so dynamic segments are written `*`, never `[orgId]`; a scanned PDF with no text layer has its first two pages rendered to images by `pdf-parse` `getScreenshot()` and read with OCR, method `ocr`, so it is never marked ready; a blank or unreadable scan is refused with `SCANNED_PDF`) and runs `extractBill()` (`lib/evidence/bill-extractor.ts`, deterministic: consumption line over readings, rates and last year, per-field confidence, alternatives, supplier, invoice number, billing period). The result is saved as an `EvidenceClassification`; no record is created until the person confirms, then the page posts the record (`dataOrigin: invoiced`, `in_review`) and attaches the bill. Uploading evidence to a record sets its evidence status to complete. **Attach bills to existing records** (records page, `match-bills.tsx`): each uploaded bill is read the same way, then `GET /api/orgs/{orgId}/evidence/{evidenceId}/matches` ranks the org's records it could evidence (`lib/evidence/match.ts`: a record is a candidate only when the quantity agrees within 2% after unit conversion; supplier, billing period overlap and category raise the score; `CONFIDENT_MATCH` 80 is preselected); nothing is attached until the person confirms. **Bill inbox** (`lib/evidence/bill-inbox.ts`, migration `20260925000041`): an editor turns on `<token>@BILL_INBOX_DOMAIN` (`Organization.billInboxToken`, `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/bill-inbox`, rotatable); Resend receives the mail and calls `POST /api/webhooks/resend-inbound` (`email.received`, Svix-signed with `RESEND_INBOUND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, checked by `verifySvix()`); only mail from a current member in `INBOX_SENDER_ROLES` is accepted (others are audit-logged as `evidence.inbox_rejected`); PDF/photo attachments up to 10 MB are fetched from `GET /emails/receiving/{id}/attachments` and stored as evidence with a `BillInboxItem` (deduped per email and file). They are read and matched on the records page like uploads, then attached or dismissed. Needs `BILL_INBOX_DOMAIN`, `RESEND_INBOUND_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and `RESEND_API_KEY` on Vercel plus a Resend receiving domain and webhook; without `BILL_INBOX_DOMAIN` the inbox is hidden.

**Employee commuting** (`lib/commuting/`, sidebar → Data → Commuting): Scope 3 Category 7, distance-based, from site attendance and each site's anonymous travel survey. No home postcode is asked for or read (GDPR). `POST /api/orgs/{orgId}/commuting/attendance` reads a CSV/Excel sign-in export (MSite, Biosite, Sitemetric or a spreadsheet; `parseAttendance()` finds the date, person and employer columns, ignores any postcode column and says so, reads text dates day-first because `xlsx` is called with `raw: true`, counts one day per person per date) in memory and never stores it. Without `confirm` it returns a preview so the person ticks which employers are their own staff; with it, `importAttendance()` makes one `CommuteImport` per site and month (migration `20260925000043`, unique per site and month, deletable while none of its records is approved or calculated) and records per mode (`s3-commuting`, km, `transportMode` "Car"/"BEV"/"Motorbike"/"Bus"/"Rail" as matched by `factor-hint.test.ts`; `calculated`, `in_review`, `siteId`/`contractId`), with a CSV of days and survey averages by mode as evidence. Distance: the survey (`CommuteSurvey`/`CommuteSurveyResponse`, public page `/commute/{token}`, `POST /api/public/commute/{token}`, rate-limited, no identifying fields) asks mode, people in the vehicle, employer and the round trip home to site in miles (`roundTripMiles`, 0 to `MAX_ROUND_TRIP_MILES` 300, stored as `roundTripKm`, migration `20260927000052`; older answers have none and only count as people). `surveyDistances()` averages each answer's round trip at its own mode (car/van/BEV/motorbike factors are per vehicle.km, so divided by occupancy) into km per mode per day on site, from own staff's answers when there are `MIN_SURVEY_RESPONSES` (5) with a distance, else everyone's; with fewer the import is refused (`NOT_ENOUGH_ANSWERS`) rather than assume a distance. Vans use the average car factor (no van commuting factor in the library) and the note says so. Only own staff enter the inventory; subcontractor km (their answers, else everyone's) are kept in the import summary for project (PAS 2080 A5) views, never recorded.
