# MetricOra personas

These are hypotheses until interviews confirm them. No interview notes exist yet, so every quote below is invented to be tested, not heard.

Assumption: in UK SME contractors carbon usually sits with the SHEQ (safety, health, environment, quality) manager rather than a dedicated sustainability manager. Test this in the first five interviews.

## 1. Gareth Holroyd, SHEQ Manager (technical user)

**Identity.** 44, Wakefield. SHEQ Manager at a civils and groundworks contractor, 160 staff, about £40m turnover, family owned, bidding for highways and council frameworks. Salary: Estimate £55k to £65k. UK SHEQ managers average about £51k to £60k, and construction adverts run £50k to £70k plus car ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/sheq-manager-salary-SRCH_KO0,12.htm), [Reed](https://www.reed.co.uk/average-salary/average-sheq-manager-salary), [Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sheq-manager)).

**Day in the life.** Starts at 7am with a site visit, an accident report and a permit query. Afternoons go on ISO 14001 and 45001 paperwork in SharePoint and Excel, and on chasing site managers for fuel and waste figures by email. Carbon is about 20% of his job and 80% of his irritation. The work arrives in bursts: when the SECR deadline lands, a tender needs a Carbon Reduction Plan, or a Tier 1 client emails a supplier questionnaire.

**Goals.**
- Primary: get this year's SECR figures and Carbon Reduction Plan done without a consultant before the accounts close.
- Secondary: stop re-keying waste tickets and fuel receipts every quarter.
- Motivation: professional pride in being audit-proof. He has been caught out once by a client asking where a number came from.

**Pain points.**
- "Every site manager sends fuel in a different format, and half the waste tickets are photos in my WhatsApp."
- "The client asked where our Scope 3 figure came from and I couldn't show them."
- "I rebuild the same spreadsheet every year and I'm never sure I used the right DEFRA year."

**Current workarounds.**
- Excel workbook with DEFRA factors pasted in. It breaks when factors change, and no one else can audit it. Estimate: 4 hours a week, from 2 days a month of chasing and keying spread across the year.
- Consultant for the CRP or SECR. Estimate: £1,500 to £3,000 a year, from the £1,999 entry price of [Compare Your Footprint](https://small99.co.uk/partners/compare-your-footprint/) and typical consultant day rates.

**Decision-making.**
- Discovers tools through Google searches ("PPN 006 template", "DEFRA factors 2026"), IEMA and CHAS newsletters, and peers at framework supplier days.
- Tries it if it has a free trial on his own data and a field app his site managers will actually use.
- Pays when a tender with a PPN 006 requirement or a Tier 1 questionnaire is due within 6 weeks.
- Other buyers: MD signs off anything over about £1k a year; the FD checks cost.

**Product fit.** Urgency 4, willingness to pay 3, reachability 4. **Primary.**

## 2. Priya Chandran, Bid Manager (acute user)

**Identity.** 36, Birmingham. Bid Manager at a regional FM contractor (cleaning, security and M&E maintenance), 900 staff, about £60m turnover, bidding for NHS trusts and central government frameworks. Salary: £48k to £60k. UK bid managers average £47.6k to £50.6k ([Indeed](https://uk.indeed.com/career/bid-manager/salaries), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/bid-manager-salary-SRCH_KO0,11.htm)), and built-environment roles run £55k to £75k+ ([Calibrate](https://calibraterecruitment.co.uk/blog/bid-coordinator-salary-uk-2026/)).

**Day in the life.** Works across several tender portals, a bid library in SharePoint and a Monday bid/no-bid meeting. Every quality submission has a sustainability or social value question at 10 to 20% weighting. She has to chase three departments for the numbers, and they come back in different units.

**Goals.**
- Primary: hit scores on carbon and social value questions and pass the PPN 006 gate on two big bids this quarter.
- Secondary: build a reusable answer library that stays accurate.
- Motivation: her win rate is her reputation and her bonus.

**Pain points.**
- "I get the Carbon Reduction Plan the night before submission and it doesn't match the figure we gave in the last bid."
- "Social value is in one system, carbon in another, and the evaluator wants both for the same contract."
- "I can't write 'we reduced emissions' without a number someone will stand behind."

**Current workarounds.**
- Bid library in Word or Loopio holding old carbon answers. They go stale, and she re-verifies each one. Estimate: 3 to 5 hours per bid, at about 2 bids a month.
- Social Value Portal for TOMs. It is separate from carbon, and winners pay a percentage of contract value ([help](https://www.socialvalueportal.com/help-and-support)).

**Decision-making.**
- Discovers tools through APMP UK, LinkedIn bid groups and freelance bid writers.
- Tries it if she can see a sample bid pack, and if it produces answers built from real figures, not AI text.
- Pays when she loses marks on sustainability in a debrief, or when a portal makes the CRP a pass/fail gate.
- Other buyers: Head of Bids and the Operations Director hold the budget; SHEQ owns the data.

**Product fit.** Urgency 5, willingness to pay 3, reachability 4. **Secondary.**

## 3. Mark Tennant, Managing Director (economic buyer)

**Identity.** 57, Harrogate. Managing Director and part-owner of a building contractor, 90 staff, about £25m turnover, with a growing share of public work. Income: Not found. Director pay at this size varies too widely to cite.

**Day in the life.** Mornings on cashflow, subcontractor disputes and the pipeline review. He reads Construction News and hears about net zero from clients and the bank. He signs the Carbon Reduction Plan declaration without being sure what is in it, which bothers him.

**Goals.**
- Primary: keep qualifying for public frameworks, which now make up 40% of the pipeline in this persona.
- Secondary: answer the bank and the insurer about climate risk.
- Motivation: protect the business and his name. He does not want to sign something that turns out to be wrong.

**Pain points.**
- "I'm asked to sign a declaration I can't check."
- "We pay a consultant every year and start from scratch every year."
- "If Gareth leaves, the carbon numbers leave with him."

**Current workarounds.**
- An annual consultant. Cost: Estimate £2,000 to £5,000 a year, within Seedling's stated SME range of £3,000 to £15,000 ([Seedling](https://www.seedling.earth/post/carbon-accounting-software-pricing)).
- Relying on one staff member. The risk is hard to price, but real.

**Decision-making.**
- Discovers tools through peers at Build UK and CECA events, and through his accountant or bank.
- Tries it if a peer contractor uses it, or through a pilot on one live project.
- Pays when a lost framework place or a client audit exposes a gap. Tender value dwarfs £3k a year.
- Other buyers: FD; the SHEQ manager as champion.

**Product fit.** Urgency 3, willingness to pay 5, reachability 2. **Tertiary.**
