Most carbon accounting platforms profit from opacity.

A sustainability manager uploads data to the platform, clicks "calculate," and receives a PDF report with totals and charts. When the auditor asks how those totals were derived, the platform's response is: "Trust us. Our model is proprietary."

This is not unreasonable caution. It's the business model. If your calculation methodology is secret, customers can't easily compare you to competitors. Switching costs stay high. Vendor lock-in persists.

CarbonSite's approach is the opposite: our methodology is public. You can audit our calculations on GitHub.

## Why Open Methodology Builds Trust

An immutable principle of financial accounting is reproducibility. If an auditor challenges a number, you provide the formula, the inputs, and a walk-through of the calculation. Carbon accounting should work the same way.

When your calculation methodology is proprietary, you're asking auditors to trust a black box. That works for some platforms, but it creates friction for others. A CFO or board might push back: "How do we know the formula is correct? What if the vendor's factor library is outdated? What if there's a bug we can't see?"

With CarbonSite, the formula is visible. The factor selection logic is visible. The versioning is visible. If an auditor has a question, we can point to the exact code or the exact factor that produced a number.

This is not weakness. It's a trust multiplier.

## What "Open Methodology" Actually Means

CarbonSite publishes:

1. **Factor Library** — DEFRA 2025, EPA 2025, and community factors as searchable CSV. Download the factors we use. Verify they match your expectations. Audit our selections.

2. **Calculation Formulas** — Gas-specific emissions calculations (CO2 + CH4×GWP + N2O×GWP) stored with each result. Auditors can reproduce totals exactly.

3. **Methodology Versioning** — When we update methodology, the version is recorded with the calculation. A report published with methodology v2026-01 used different assumptions than methodology v2027-01. Auditors can see the change.

4. **Source Code** — Our calculation engine is on GitHub. Developers can audit the code. Security researchers can look for bugs. Customers can run the code locally if they want.

5. **Transparency Report** — We publish annually how our factor library is maintained, which external sources we use, when we updated assumptions.

## The Competitive Advantage

This sounds risky. Shouldn't hiding the methodology give you an advantage?

In the short term, maybe. Vendor lock-in is real.

In the long term, no. Customers are starting to care more about defensibility than convenience. A finance lead who needs carbon numbers to withstand board scrutiny will choose a platform they can audit over one that's a black box.

Enterprise platforms like Normative and Persefoni profit from complexity, not from opacity. The reason they're expensive is not that their calculations are secret. It's that their sales process is complex and their integrations are deep.

CarbonSite's advantage is clarity. A customer can evaluate our methodology, decide it's sound, and trust the numbers because they understand how they were derived.

## Real Example: Scope 2 Electricity Emissions

Here's what transparency looks like in practice.

A facility's electricity consumption is 100 MWh for a month. The facility is in California (which uses mostly renewables). If we use location-based method:

**CarbonSite's approach:**

1. Take the consumption: 100 MWh
2. Look up California's grid factor: 0.15 kg CO2e per kWh (available publicly from eGrid)
3. Calculate: 100 MWh × 1,000 kWh/MWh × 0.15 kg CO2e/kWh = 15,000 kg CO2e
4. Store the formula: `100 × 1000 × 0.15` and the factor source (eGrid 2025)
5. Record methodology version: v2026-01
6. Freeze in immutable calculation row

An auditor can see every step. They can verify the eGrid factor is current. They can see exactly when the methodology was updated. They can reproduce the math.

**A proprietary platform's approach:**

1. Take the consumption: 100 MWh
2. Click "calculate"
3. Result: 15,000 kg CO2e
4. When auditor asks for the factor and formula: "Contact our sales team"

The numbers might be identical. The trust is not.

## How This Changes Your Reporting

When your calculation methodology is transparent, your audit preparation is simpler:

- Auditors trust the formulas because they can see them
- You don't need a spreadsheet to "explain" the numbers
- Your factor selections are defensible because they're documented
- Version control means you can show exactly what changed between reporting periods

This reduces audit friction and time. Auditors can focus on data quality and governance, not questioning the math.
