On 5 August 2026 the Cabinet Office published **Procurement Policy Note 026: The Social Value Model**. It replaces the approach in PPN 002 for central government buyers and narrows social value to one question: does this contract create good jobs and build skills? This post covers what changed and what a contractor should set up before the first PPN 026 tender.

## What PPN 026 covers

The model has **two outcomes and six award criteria**:

| Outcome | Award criteria |
|---|---|
| **Good Jobs**: good employment opportunities in every postcode | Creating and retaining high quality jobs; fair working conditions; fair pay |
| **Skills**: learning and development that addresses skills needs and shortages | Training and retraining; in-work progression; talent pipeline |

Buyers award points for going beyond the statutory minimum and for supporting people who face barriers to work. Apprenticeships, work placements and pre-employment training all sit under Skills.

PPN 002 had 17 award criteria across eight policy outcomes. PPN 026 has six across two.

## When it applies and how it is weighted

- **Who:** central government departments, for procurements that start on or after **1 January 2027**.
- **Threshold:** a total contract value of **£1 million or more, including VAT**. PPN 002 applied from the standard above-threshold level, so PPN 026 catches fewer, larger contracts.
- **Weighting:** at least **10%** of the total score for contracts under £5 million, and at least **20%** at £5 million and above.

Detailed guidance on sub-criteria, the evaluation method and target groups was due in autumn 2026. Check each tender's documents for the version it uses.

## What it no longer covers

Carbon, the environment and community wellbeing are no longer part of central government social value. Carbon is still assessed separately: bidders for central government contracts of £5 million a year or more usually need a **Carbon Reduction Plan under PPN 006** ([our guide](/blog/ppn-006-carbon-reduction-plan-guide)).

Local authorities, NHS bodies and housing associations are not bound by PPN 026 and many still use the National TOMs framework. A contractor bidding across the public sector will often report PPN 026 KPIs, National TOMs and a PPN 006 plan in the same year.

## Delivery is measured through KPIs

PPN 026 sets **no standard reporting metrics**. What you commit to in the bid becomes contract KPIs that the buyer's contract manager monitors. For contracts of £5 million or more, departments are expected to include **at least three** social value KPIs.

In practice that shifts the work from writing the answer to evidencing it for the life of the contract. A commitment such as "12 apprenticeship starts on the contract" needs:

1. **A KPI with a number and a unit**, linked to the award criterion it answers (here, Training and retraining).
2. **A delivery log** with a date and quantity for each start.
3. **Evidence for each entry**, such as the apprenticeship agreement, a training record or a payroll extract.
4. **A reviewer's approval**, so the figure you report to the contract manager is one you can show.

## A checklist for your first PPN 026 bid

- Decide which of the six criteria you can actually deliver on this contract. Do not commit to all six.
- Write each commitment as a KPI with a target, a unit and a date.
- Agree with the site team who records delivery and what evidence they keep.
- Keep the carbon answer separate: PPN 006 figures come from your published inventory, not from the social value section.
- For bids to local authorities or the NHS, check whether they ask for National TOMs instead.
