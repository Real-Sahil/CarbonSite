On most civils sites, red diesel and HVO in excavators, dumpers and generators are the biggest part of Scope 1. A share of that fuel is burnt with the engine running and the machine doing nothing.

## What the research measured

Phil Lewis, Michael Leming and William Rasdorf studied this with field data from 34 items of nonroad diesel construction equipment ([Lewis, Leming and Rasdorf, *Journal of Management in Engineering* 28(1), 31–38, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)ME.1943-5479.0000068)). They defined a machine's *operational efficiency* as working (non-idle) time divided by total engine time. They then modelled how fuel use and CO₂ change as that ratio falls. The same team also reported the effect of idling on air-quality pollutants from the same kind of equipment ([Lewis et al., *Transportation Research Record* 2270, 2012](https://doi.org/10.3141/2270-09)).

Their measure is useful because it needs only two numbers that most modern machines already record: total engine hours and idle hours.

## Measuring idling on your own sites

**Use the telematics you already have.** Most machines from the last decade report engine hours, idle hours and fuel through the manufacturer's portal. The ISO 15143-3 (AEMP 2.0) standard makes those feeds readable across brands.

**Work out operational efficiency per machine.** Divide working hours by total engine hours for each machine and period. A machine at 0.6 spent four hours in ten idling.

**Reconcile against what you bought.** Telematics fuel is an estimate from the engine. The figure in your inventory should come from delivery notes and fuel receipts. Comparing the two by site shows where the records are incomplete.

## What to do with the numbers

- Set an idle limit per machine class and review the worst performers each month.
- Check that auto-idle and auto-shutdown are switched on and not overridden.
- Plan work so machines are not left running while they wait for wagons.
- Count the saving in fuel receipts, not in telematics, so it shows up in the reported figure.
