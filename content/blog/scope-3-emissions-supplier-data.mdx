# Scope 3 Emissions: From Supplier Silence to Collaborative Data

Every quarter, a procurement manager at a mid-sized manufacturer sends out a Scope 3 data collection survey to 50 suppliers.

Expected response rate: 30%. (This is industry standard. 7 of 10 suppliers simply ignore the email.)

Expected data quality: poor. Most responses are estimates. Some suppliers forward last year's numbers. One supplier copies their energy consumption and labels it as waste emissions.

Expected time to chase: 40 hours of follow-ups, clarifications, re-sends.

Expected result: a Scope 3 footprint with a confidence interval of ±40%. Your sustainability director has to explain to the board: "We think it's 50,000 tonnes, but it could be 30,000 or 70,000."

This is the Scope 3 problem.

## Why Scope 3 Kills Carbon Accounting

Scope 3 (upstream and downstream supply chain) is typically 60–90% of a company's total emissions. But it's also the most uncertain.

Here's why:

1. **You don't own the data.** Your suppliers do. Their accounting systems, their measurement methods, their data quality.
2. **Suppliers don't prioritize it.** They got 50 emails last month asking for carbon data. Yours wasn't urgent.
3. **The medium sucks.** Email → spreadsheet → email again = transcription errors, version confusion, data loss.
4. **Estimation is vague.** "We bought €100k of raw materials from your supplier in Italy." From that, carbon accountants extrapolate: €100k × Italian average emission factor for that industry = Scope 3 emissions. Could be ±50% off.

Result: your total carbon footprint is essentially a guess with high confidence and low accuracy.

CFOs hate this. Auditors hate this. SBTi validators reject this.

## The Traditional "Solution": Supplier Surveys

Platforms like Watershed and Persefoni have built enterprise supplier portals. The idea: give suppliers a web interface to self-report their emissions.

Outcome: lukewarm.

Response rates are 30–50%. Quality is inconsistent. And you still need procurement staff to chase suppliers for corrections.

Why? Because filling out a carbon survey isn't a supplier's job. Their job is delivering products, managing orders, hitting delivery dates. A carbon survey is overhead they didn't ask for.

## The Estimation Engine: Filling the Gaps

Not every supplier will capture emissions. Some are small, uncooperative, or simply lack smartphones.

For those, CarbonSite's Scope 3 estimation engine steps in.

It uses:
- **Spend data** (how much you bought from this supplier)
- **Supplier attributes** (industry, country, company size)
- **Historical patterns** (how similar suppliers' emissions correlate to spend)
- **Linear regression** (trained on supplier data you *do* have)

Result: a predicted emissions value with a confidence score.

Example: "Based on your €50k spend with this Italian component supplier, we estimate 2,500 tonnes CO₂e (confidence: 78%). Similar suppliers in this industry average 2,100–2,900 tonnes."

Is it perfect? No. Is it better than assuming zero emissions? Yes.

And critically: it's **reproducible**. You can explain to auditors exactly how the estimate was derived. No black box. No magic.

## The Compliance Story: SBTi and CSRD

SBTi (Science-Based Targets initiative) requires validated Scope 3 data. The validator will ask: "How do you verify supplier emissions?"

Option A (traditional): "We send surveys, most don't respond, we guess."
Validator: "Rejected."

Option B (CarbonSite): "We capture actual emissions from field operations when suppliers deliver. For suppliers who don't report, we use a documented estimation model trained on supplier spend."
Validator: "Approved."

CSRD similarly requires you to measure Scope 3 "to the extent possible." Actual measurement (field capture) beats estimation. Documented estimation beats assumptions.
