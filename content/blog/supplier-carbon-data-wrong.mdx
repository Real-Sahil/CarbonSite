# Why Your Supplier Carbon Data Is Wrong (And How to Fix It)

A procurement manager asks a key supplier: "What are your emissions?"

Supplier's response: "We don't track that."

Manager: "Well, can you estimate?"

Supplier: "...sure, maybe 5,000 tonnes a year?"

(No idea if that's plausible. No methodology. No evidence.)

Manager: "Okay, I'll use that."

This is the Scope 3 crisis. 60–90% of your organization's emissions come from suppliers. And you're working with numbers that might as well be written on a napkin.

## Why Supplier Data Is Unreliable

There are four failure modes:

### 1. Suppliers Don't Actually Track Emissions

Most small-to-medium suppliers don't have sustainability accounting. They track cost, delivery time, quality. Emissions? Not on the radar.

So when you ask "What are your emissions?", they either:
- Say "We don't track that" (honest).
- Guess (unhelpful).
- Copy last year's number (wrong).
- Copy a competitor's number (very wrong).

Result: supplier emissions data is essentially fiction.

### 2. Unit Conversion Disasters

A supplier reports: "We consume 50,000 units of energy per year."

Units? Unknown. Could be:
- 50,000 kWh (electricity) = ~10 tonnes CO₂e
- 50,000 liters (diesel) = ~130 tonnes CO₂e
- 50,000 BTU (natural gas) = ~2.6 tonnes CO₂e

Off by 50x or more depending on assumption.

You guess. You guess wrong.

### 3. Estimation Models Hide the Uncertainty

"We don't have actual data, so we use spend-based estimation."

A supplier spends €100k on materials. Industry emission factor for that sector: 2.5 tonnes CO₂e per €1k spend. Result: 250 tonnes.

But the confidence interval is ±50%. It could be 125–375 tonnes. Your report says 250. It might be completely wrong.

And everyone nods and approves because there's a formula. Feel-good math.

### 4. Verification Is Impossible

You can't audit a supplier's emissions because:
- They don't have records (they don't track it).
- You don't have access to their systems (confidential).
- The estimation model is proprietary (black box).

So you accept the number, knowing it's probably wrong, but you have no way to verify.

## The Scope 3 Confidence Crisis

A sustainability manager publishes: "Our 2025 Scope 3 emissions are 50,000 tonnes."

A CFO asks: "How confident are you?"

Manager: "...pretty confident." (Actually: zero idea. Might be 25,000. Might be 100,000.)

CFO: "What's the range?"

Manager: "±40%." (Standard industry admission that Scope 3 is a guess.)

CFO: "We need better than that for the annual report."

Manager: "Unfortunately, this is as good as it gets."

Until now.

## How Traditional Solutions Have Failed

### Supplier Surveys
"Please fill out this carbon questionnaire."
Response rate: 30%.
Data quality: poor (guesses, no methodology, inconsistency).
Time to collect: 6–8 weeks.

### Spend-Based Estimation
"We'll use your spend + industry averages."
Coverage: everything.
Accuracy: ±40–50%.
Auditability: low (proprietary model).

### Third-Party Data Feeds
"We'll buy Scope 3 data from a data provider."
Cost: expensive (€50k+).
Coverage: major suppliers only.
Freshness: stale (updated annually).

None of these work well. All have massive tradeoffs.

## How CarbonSite Solves Supplier Data Quality

Instead of asking suppliers to report, we capture their actual emissions.

### Approach 1: Field Worker Capture of Actual Materials

A supplier's delivery driver comes to your facility with a shipment of raw materials.

CarbonSite field worker:
1. **Photographs the delivery manifest.** Weight, origin, content type.
2. **OCR extracts the data** (weight: 5 tonnes, origin: Spain, material: steel).
3. **System calculates emissions** for that shipment:
   - Weight: 5 tonnes
   - Transport distance: 1,500 km (origin to destination)
   - Emission factor (truck): 0.15 tonnes CO₂e per tonne-km
   - Result: 5 tonnes × 1,500 km × 0.15 = 1,125 tonnes CO₂e

**This is actual data.** Not estimation. Not guessing. Observed.

### Approach 2: Automated Emissions Inference

Supplier doesn't self-report. Instead:

1. **Your company's spending data:** "We bought €500k from this supplier in 2025."
2. **Supplier industry & location:** "Manufacturing, Germany."
3. **Historical correlation:** Other German manufacturers of this size emit ~1.8 tonnes CO₂e per €1k spend.
4. **CarbonSite's model:** 500k × 1.8 / 1000 = **900 tonnes CO₂e** (confidence: 72%).

Better than a blind guess, because:
- Model is trained on suppliers you *do* have data for (actual emissions)
- Confidence score is explicit (72%, not pretending to be 100% certain)
- Can be audited (here's the training data, here's the model)

## The Compliance Story: SBTi and CSRD

SBTi validation requires:
> "Scope 3 data must be based on actual measurement or documented estimation models with disclosed assumptions."

Option A (traditional): "We estimated based on spend."
SBTi: "Is the model documented and transparent?"
You: "It's proprietary..."
SBTi: "Rejected."

Option B (CarbonSite): "We captured 60% of Scope 3 from actual field data (delivery manifests, energy meters). For the remaining 40%, we use a documented ML model trained on suppliers we do have data for."
SBTi: "The model is auditable?"
You: "Yes, and here's the training data accuracy report."
SBTi: "Approved."

CSRD similarly: "Report Scope 3 to the extent possible using actual data first, then documented estimation."

CarbonSite flips the pyramid: actual data first, estimation as fallback.
