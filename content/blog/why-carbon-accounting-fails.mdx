Every quarter, sustainability teams across the UK face the same frustrating problem: asking field workers, site managers, and contractors to fill out spreadsheets about emissions.

The result? Mountains of inconsistent data. Weight recorded in "tonnes" on one form, "kilograms" on another. Dates missing entirely. Supplier names abbreviated differently each time. And by the time your team realizes the data is unusable, three weeks have passed, your carbon reporting deadline is looming, and you're back to square one.

This isn't incompetence. It's a structural failure of how most carbon accounting platforms work.

## The Spreadsheet Problem

Here's how traditional carbon accounting platforms handle data collection:

1. **Accounting team creates a CSV template** — columns for date, weight, supplier, cost
2. **Field workers email back half-filled spreadsheets** — often weeks late
3. **Finance team spends days cleaning data** — reformatting, guessing at missing values, checking for duplicates
4. **Errors slip through** — a facility records 500 tonnes of waste when they meant 50, and nobody notices until the report is published
5. **Auditors push back** — "Where's the evidence? How do you know this is real?"

The problem is that spreadsheets assume the person filling them out knows:
- Exact unit conversions (is it net or gross weight?)
- Which emission factors apply (are they using the latest DEFRA conversion?)
- How to structure data for downstream calculation

They don't. And they shouldn't have to.

## The Field Worker's Reality

Talk to a site manager at a construction firm, a recycling centre operator, or a logistics coordinator, and they'll tell you the same thing:

*"We're not carbon experts. We're trying to run a business. When someone asks us to fill out a carbon form, we don't know if we're doing it right. We estimate, we guess, and we hope it's good enough."*

This is where field capture comes in. Instead of asking a worker to *remember* what happened on site and then *interpret* it for a carbon accounting system, you let them do what they're already doing: photograph the evidence.

- **Waste ticket with weight?** Photograph it.
- **Delivery note from supplier?** Photograph it.
- **Fuel receipt for the vehicle?** Photograph it.
- **Meter reading at the facility?** Photograph it.

The system does the rest.

When data comes from photographs instead of spreadsheets, something unexpected happens: transparency becomes possible.

An internal stakeholder can:
