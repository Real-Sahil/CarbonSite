# Why Carbon Accounting Still Fails: A Field Worker's Perspective

Last month, a logistics manager at a mid-sized waste disposal company forwarded me an email from their sustainability director: "We need your crew to stop writing weights on the back of tickets. Start using the web portal to log each trip."

The crew never logged a single entry.

Three weeks later, the director had 47 unreviewed submissions in the backlog. Half were incomplete. One was a typo so egregious—2,000 tonnes instead of 200 kg—that it would have skewed the year's entire carbon report.

This is the hidden crisis in carbon accounting. Most platforms work perfectly... if you have perfect data. But carbon accounting doesn't start in a cloud dashboard. It starts in a truck bed, on a handwritten receipt, in the rain.

## The Field Data Nightmare

Here's what actually happens at the source:

1. **A waste ticket is issued.** By hand. Font size varies, numbers are handwritten, sometimes smudged.
2. **A driver or site manager looks at it.** They estimate the weight. Or they forget to record it at all.
3. **Data gets typed into a spreadsheet.** Days later. Sometimes transcription errors slip in (decimal points, unit conversions).
4. **The spreadsheet gets emailed.** To a contractor, a supplier, a corporate office. Each forward risks formatting corruption.
5. **A sustainability person manually reviews it.** Flag outliers, chase down clarifications, chase down people for corrections.
6. **Finally, it enters the carbon accounting system.** If it survives all that, it's probably wrong anyway.

And this is the *simple* path. For Scope 3 data (supplier emissions), multiply this process by 50 suppliers who all have their own systems, timelines, and (honestly) motivation levels.

The platforms? They're perfectly designed for step 5 onwards. Dashboard, review queue, calculation engine, report. Beautiful charts. But step 1–4? That's seen as the client's problem to solve. Bring us clean data, and we'll track it.

## The Math on Lost Hours

A typical mid-market organization with 10 facilities:
- 5 people-hours/week on data collection
- 3 people-hours/week on data quality review
- 2 people-hours/week on corrections and re-submissions

That's **600 hours/year** spent hunting data and fixing mistakes. For a sustainability manager billing at €80/hour, that's €48,000 in sunk cost. Every single year.

And the data *still* ends up wrong.

Bad data corrupts everything downstream.

A single data entry error—one unit conversion mistake, one misread weight—cascades through your report. If that entry is Scope 3 (supplier emissions), it could skew your entire carbon footprint by 5–10%.

CFOs and auditors know this. They flag data quality as their top concern in carbon accounting surveys.

## The Audit Readiness Advantage

Here's what auditors actually care about:

1. **Evidence.** Do you have a photograph of the source document?
2. **Trace.** Can you explain where the number came from?
3. **Reproducibility.** If I recalculate using your methodology, do I get the same result?
4. **Immutability.** Can anyone retroactively change the record?

Field capture + GPS tagging + timestamps + append-only logs = all four.

Traditional platforms? They've got 2 and 3. Maybe 1 if you upload the receipt yourself (extra step).
