The GHG Protocol Corporate Standard splits an organisation's emissions into three scopes, so that each tonne is counted once and by the right party. For a contractor, drawing the line is mostly a question of **who controls the activity**.

## Scope 1: direct emissions

Sources the company owns or controls.

| Source | Typical data | Factor (DEFRA 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Diesel in plant and vehicles | fuel receipts, bulk deliveries (litres) | 2.58354 kg CO₂e per litre (average biofuel blend) |
| Gas oil in generators | delivery notes (litres) | per litre, gas oil |
| HVO | fuel receipts (litres) | 0.03558 kg CO₂e per litre, biogenic CO₂ reported separately |
| Natural gas in offices and depots | gas bills (kWh) | 0.18231 kg CO₂e per kWh (gross CV) |
| Refrigerant leaks | service records (kg topped up) | the gas's global warming potential |

Fuel is often the largest Scope 1 line. Idle time is a large, avoidable part of it: see [idling plant](/blog/plant-idling-fuel-and-carbon).

## Scope 2: purchased energy

Electricity, heat, steam and cooling that the company buys and uses. That includes temporary builders' supplies on site and permanent supplies at offices and yards.

Scope 2 is reported two ways. **Location-based** uses the grid average: 0.13096 kg CO₂e per kWh in the 2026 UK factors. **Market-based** reflects your contracts, such as REGO-backed tariffs and PPAs, with a residual mix for the rest. Headline totals normally use one method and show the other beside it. See [location-based and market-based Scope 2](/blog/location-and-market-based-scope-2) and [what changed in the 2026 factors](/blog/uk-ghg-conversion-factors-2026-what-changed).

## Scope 3: the value chain

Everything else the company causes but does not control. For contractors the material categories are usually:

- **Category 1, purchased goods and services**: concrete, steel, aggregates, asphalt, timber, and subcontracted labour and services. Often the largest part of the footprint. Supplier or quantity data is best; spend-based factors are the fallback. See [spend-based Scope 3 with factors you can source](/blog/spend-based-scope-3-sourced-factors).
- **Category 2, capital goods**: plant and vehicles bought.
- **Category 4, upstream transportation**: deliveries to site you pay for.
- **Category 5, waste generated in operations**: skips and muck-away, from waste transfer notes and weighbridge tickets. See [from a waste ticket to a reported tonne](/blog/waste-ticket-to-reported-tonne).
- **Categories 6 and 7, business travel and employee commuting**.

The embodied carbon of materials installed on a project is also tracked project by project under PAS 2080: see [embodied carbon and data quality](/blog/embodied-carbon-data-quality).

## Drawing the boundary

- **Choose a consolidation approach**, usually operational control, and apply it to joint ventures and subsidiaries.
- **Decide how hired plant is treated.** Fuel you buy for hired plant is usually your Scope 1. Fuel a subcontractor buys is your Scope 3.
- **Write it down.** The boundary statement is the first thing a verifier reads.

## Which scopes are required

| Framework | Scopes |
|---|---|
| SECR | Scope 1 and 2 from UK energy use, see [our SECR guide](/blog/secr-reporting-guide-for-contractors) |
| PPN 006 Carbon Reduction Plan | Scope 1, 2 and Scope 3 categories 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, see [our PPN 006 guide](/blog/ppn-006-carbon-reduction-plan-guide) |
| SBTi near-term targets | Scope 1 and 2, plus Scope 3 when it is 40% or more of the total |

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Scope 1 for a contractor?** Fuel burnt in plant, generators and vehicles you control, gas in your buildings, and refrigerant leaks.

**What is Scope 2?** Purchased electricity, heat and cooling, reported location-based and market-based.

**What is Scope 3?** The rest of the value chain: materials, subcontractors, waste, transport, travel and commuting.

**Is hired plant Scope 1 or 3?** It depends on who controls the fuel; state your rule.
