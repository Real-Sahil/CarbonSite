Carbon accounting is broken at the capture layer.

Most platforms assume data arrives pre-cleaned from accounting systems, spreadsheets, or utility bills. The reality is messier. A sustainability manager at a construction company receives dozens of waste tickets from subcontractors every week, each with different formats, incomplete information, and handwritten notes. A logistics coordinator gets delivery dockets from suppliers with weights in different units (kilograms, tonnes, pounds). A facility manager has utility consumption data that arrives in multiple formats from multiple buildings.

By the time this data reaches a carbon accounting platform, it's already been transcribed multiple times, corrupted through manual entry, and separated from its source documents.

This is not a software problem. It's an operational problem.

## The Spreadsheet Trap

Most organizations start with a spreadsheet. A sustainability team creates a simple template: facility, category, amount, unit, date. Within months, it grows to five spreadsheets. By year two, there's a master sheet, a draft, a "corrected" version, and an "old methodology" archive. By year three, the audit trail is gone.

The problem is that spreadsheets separate data from context. The original weight might be written on a waste ticket with a handprint signature, stored in a supplier's cloud drive, and transcribed by whoever happened to be available. Nobody can reproduce the original value if the spreadsheet number is challenged.

## Why Field-First Capture Changes This

Field workers are the first responders to carbon data. They're the ones photographing waste tickets, scanning delivery notes, reading meters. If you can capture emissions data at that moment with a mobile phone, you create a defensible evidence chain:

1. **Original source:** Photo of the ticket, with timestamp and GPS coordinates
2. **Extracted data:** OCR pulls weight, category, date from the image
3. **Review layer:** Sustainability manager confirms the extraction or corrects it
4. **Immutable record:** Original photo + extraction + review decision all stored together

No transcription. No manual entry. No lost context.

## OCR Isn't Magic, But It's Better Than Humans

On-device OCR has gotten remarkably good at extracting structured data from unstructured documents. It's not 100% accurate, but it's more consistent than a human transcriber. And when it makes errors, you can see exactly where it went wrong because the original image is preserved.

Here's a real example:

A delivery note shows "2,450 kg" in the weight field. A human transcriber might read this as "2,450" and forget the unit. OCR extracts both: ["2450", "kg"]. The system normalizes to tonnes: 2.45. The original photo is attached for audit.

If an auditor questions this number, you can point to the original photo and the OCR extraction. No "I think I remember someone saying..."

## Anomaly Detection Catches Errors Before They Escalate

Even with good OCR, data quality issues slip through. A facility reports 50 tonnes of waste in one week when historical average is 2 tonnes. A supplier invoice shows 1,000 liters when previous shipments showed 50 liters.

Most systems flag these with an alert. CarbonSite explains them. Was the facility undergoing renovation (explaining the spike)? Did the supplier change shipping routes (explaining the larger order)? Or is this an actual error in transcription or calculation?

Anomaly detection becomes an investigation tool, not just a warning light.

## The Governance Layer

Field capture + OCR + anomaly detection creates a governance framework:

1. **Capture:** Field worker photographs source document (immutable)
2. **Extraction:** OCR extracts data automatically
3. **Validation:** System flags anomalies or missing data
4. **Review:** Sustainability manager confirms or corrects
5. **Calculation:** Calculation engine applies factors and formulas
6. **Publication:** Immutable snapshot created for report
7. **Audit:** Every step is traceable

If an auditor asks why a number changed between reporting periods, you can walk backwards through this entire chain. You can show the original photo, explain the OCR result, show what the reviewer changed, and explain why they changed it.

This is what audit-ready carbon accounting looks like.
